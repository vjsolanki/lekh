---
title: Give the canvas a height
description: "Two ways to make a long email scroll, and why a height in `style` is not one of them."
---

## What you'll do

Decide where the scrolling lives, so a long email scrolls and the outlines stay
on the Blocks they belong to.

## The short version

Two options. Both are fine.

```tsx
// The canvas scrolls itself.
<Canvas height="100vh" />
```

```tsx
// Your layout scrolls it.
<div style={{ height: "100vh", overflowY: "auto" }}>
  <Canvas />
</div>
```

There is a third thing that looks like it should work and does not:

```tsx
<Canvas style={{ height: "100vh" }} /> // ✗ clips the chrome at the fold
```

## Which one to pick

Use **`height`** when the canvas is the whole pane. Nothing else is in the
scrolling region, and you would rather not think about it.

Use **your own scroller** when something else has to move with the email: a
caption under it, a background that scrolls with the sheet, anything you would
rather not have to keep in step yourself.

Both keep the outlines welded to their Blocks. Pick on layout, not behaviour.

The example editor uses `height`, so its measure and its crop marks stay put and
read as a window onto the email — the email moves, the frame around it does not.

## Gotchas

### The scrollbar comes out of the email, not out of the room around it

`height` puts the scroller **inside** the canvas's box, so the bar and the email
share `width`. Where scrollbars are drawn inline rather than overlaid — Windows,
Linux, and macOS set to always show them — that bar takes 15px the email was
using, and the email overflows sideways by exactly that much. You get a second,
nearly-full-width scrollbar that scrolls fifteen pixels.

```tsx
<div style={{ width: 600 }}>
  <Canvas width={600} height="100%" /> {/* ✗ 600px of email, 585px of room */}
</div>
```

Only a pane pinned to exactly the email's width has this problem. Any spare room
absorbs the bar and nothing happens, which is why `height="100vh"` in an
ordinary pane needs none of this.

If your pane is deliberately the email's width, add the gutter yourself:

```tsx
const SCROLLBAR = 15;

<div style={{ width: 600 + SCROLLBAR }}>
  <Canvas width={600} height="100%" />
</div>;
```

Then paint the paper on that box rather than on the canvas, so the gutter is
paper too and the bar has something to sit on. The example editor does both.

### `height` in `style` is not the same prop

`style` lands on the canvas element, which is the sheet — so a height there
clips the chrome instead of scrolling it. See below.

### The email's own `vh` stops meaning the window

The frame is no longer a viewport, so `vh` units and `position: fixed` inside an
email do not resolve against one. No mail client supports either, so an email
relying on them was already broken where it counts.

Widths are untouched. The frame is still exactly `width` wide and media queries
still resolve against it — only its height stopped being a viewport.

## Why not a height in `style`

`style` lands on the canvas element, and the canvas element is the sheet the
email is drawn on. It is always exactly as tall as the email.

That is not an oversight. Every outline, drop indicator and toolbar is
positioned against that element with `inset: 0`. Make it 600px tall inside a
3000px email and the chrome layer is 600px tall too — so anything below the
fold is clipped away, and you get a canvas that neither scrolls nor draws.

`height` avoids this by wrapping the sheet in a scroller of its own, leaving the
sheet full height inside it.

## Why the frame does not scroll

The email renders in an iframe. That iframe is sized to its content and scrolls
nothing.

If it scrolled internally, the email would move and the chrome would not — the
chrome lives in your document, positioned from numbers measured inside the
frame. Keeping them in step would mean recomputing on every scroll event, and a
scroll event always arrives _after_ the scroll has painted. The outlines would
trail the Blocks and snap into place when you stopped.

With one scroller holding both, there is nothing to keep in step. The browser
moves them together and the numbers never change.

This is what costs the email its viewport, which is the `vh` gotcha above.

## Scrolling to a Block

You do not have to write this. The canvas answers `reveal` itself — it finds
whichever element holds the overflow, yours or its own, and scrolls it.

```ts
editor.reveal(blockId); // the canvas scrolls it into view
```

`onReveal` is still there if you want to know a reveal happened — to
flash a highlight, say. It is not what makes the scrolling work.

## Edge scrolling during a drag

Also free. Drag a Block near the top or bottom of what you can see and the
canvas keeps scrolling, so carrying a Block a long way stays one gesture.

The edge zone is measured against the visible box — your scroller's, or the
one `height` made — not against the email. Near the bottom of a 3000px email
is not the same as near the bottom of the screen, and it is the screen that
matters.
