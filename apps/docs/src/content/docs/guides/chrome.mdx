## What you'll do

Draw the marks that sit over the canvas when nothing is happening: what is
selected, what is under the pointer, what the selected Block can do, and the
two kinds of Block that have nothing to show.

## The short version

A Slot is a component you supply and the canvas positions. It hands you a
rectangle in the canvas's own coordinates and the Block that rectangle belongs
to. You draw.

<Code code={chrome} lang="tsx" title="chrome.tsx" />

```tsx
<Canvas slots={slots} />
```

The library draws none of this. A Slot you leave out simply draws nothing,
which is worth knowing before you watch it happen: an editor with no Slots at
all still drags, still selects and still types, and shows you none of it.

## Two things that will cost you an hour

**The slot layer ignores pointer events.** That is what stops an outline
swallowing a click meant for the email underneath it. Chrome that _is_ meant to
be clicked has to opt back in:

```tsx
pointerEvents: "auto";
```

A toolbar that renders correctly and does nothing when clicked is almost always
this.

**Your CSS stops at the iframe.** The email renders inside one, so the email's
own styles apply in there and yours do not. Slots are drawn outside it, in your
document, with your design tokens and your component library.

Two consequences follow, and neither has a workaround:

You draw _over_ a Block, never on it. A scrim over the Block being dragged is
possible; fading the Block itself is not.

Nothing you draw reaches the email. None of the chrome on this page appears in
the HTML you send. That is the point — it is the editor, not the message.

## Marks stand down during a drag

While a Block is in the air, the canvas stops asking for `selection`, `hover`
and `textToolbar`. They come back the moment the drag ends.

An outline around the Block being carried and a toolbar over the position it is
leaving both compete with the one mark that matters, which is where the Block
lands. This is not configurable, and you do not have to handle it — your
components are simply not called.

`emptyBlock` is the exception, and stays drawn. Every empty container is
somewhere the Block could go, which during a drag is the most useful thing on
screen. The one it stands down on is the container the drag is landing inside,
where `dropIndicator` is already drawing on the identical rectangle.

## A grip to move the Block

By default a Block drags from anywhere on it. To move it by a grip instead,
draw one in the `selection` Slot and spread `useBlockDrag`'s props onto it:

<Code code={grip} lang="tsx" title="grip.tsx" />

Drawing the grip is the whole opt-in, and it covers every Block. Once any Block
has a grip, a press on any Block's body selects it and starts no drag. If you
draw no grip at all, every Block drags from its body as before.

The grip goes when its element unmounts. A grip in `selection` is gone while
nothing is selected, and then every Block drags from its body again. To keep
Blocks grip-only then too, draw a grip in `hover` as well.

A drag from a grip lands exactly where a drag from the Block would. A `<button>`
works as a grip, so it can take focus and has a name for a screen reader.

`useBlockDrag` also returns `isDragging` and `isRefused`. Your `selection` Slot
never sees them turn true, because it stands down during a drag. They are for
a grip drawn somewhere that stays on screen, such as a row in a layers panel.

## Blocks with nothing to show

Two Slots are about a Block the canvas cannot render properly. Both are easy to
forget, because neither shows up until a real Document does.

`emptyBlock` covers two states wearing one face: a container with no children,
and a Block whose Definition rendered nothing at all. Neither has any height,
so left alone both vanish — nothing to point at, nothing to drop into, nothing
to select. The canvas gives each one a box so it stays reachable, and asks this
Slot to draw it.

It has a default, because reserved space with nothing in it reads as a layout
bug rather than as an invitation. Most editors draw one thing for both cases.
If you want to tell them apart, `useEditor().getDefinition(block.type)` says
whether the Block is a container.

`unregisteredBlock` is drawn over a Block whose type this editor has no
Definition for. It has **no** default, so without it such a Block is just a
gap. You will meet it the first time a saved Document outlives the build that
wrote it. See [Migrate saved documents](/guides/migrations/) for the other half
of that problem.

## Common problems

**Your toolbar renders but clicking does nothing.** It needs
`pointerEvents: "auto"`. The slot layer does not take the pointer.

**An outline is in the wrong place, or drifts as you scroll.** You are adding
the canvas's offset yourself. Don't — `rect` is already translated.

**Nothing is drawn when you select a Block.** No `selection` Slot. The library
ships no default for it, and the selection still happened.

**Your chrome disappears whenever a drag starts.** Working as intended. See
above.

**Your grip does nothing.** It needs `pointerEvents: "auto"`, like any chrome
meant to be pressed. A grip for a column, or for the Block somebody is typing
into, also does nothing: neither can be dragged.

**Pressing a Block drags it even though you drew a grip.** The grip is drawn in
`selection`, and nothing was selected yet, so no grip was on screen. Draw one in
`hover` as well.

**A Block in a loaded Document is invisible.** Its type has no Definition, and
you have no `unregisteredBlock` Slot to say so.

**Empty columns show a dashed box you did not write.** That is `emptyBlock`'s
default. Pass your own to replace it, or a component returning `null` to turn
it off.

## Why it works this way

Chrome is the part of an editor that most obviously belongs to your product.
Outlines, toolbars and placeholders carry your design system, and a library
that drew them would be handing you something to override rather than something
to use.
